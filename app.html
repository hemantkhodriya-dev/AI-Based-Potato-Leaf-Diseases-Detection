Potato Foliage Pathogen Guide

Visual symptom identification and field management strategies for common potato crop foliage conditions.

Optimal Foliage

Healthy Potato Foliage

Clean, vibrant green lamina without chlorosis, necrotic spotting, or water-soaked patches. Demonstrates active chlorophyll photosynthesis and healthy vascular tissue.

Recommended Field Care: Maintain balanced NPK nutrient supply, monitor soil moisture via tensiometers, and avoid excessive evening overhead irrigation.
Fungal Pathogen

Early Blight (Alternaria solani)

Fungal disease characterized by brown-to-black necrotic spots expanding into concentric target rings surrounded by chlorotic yellow halos. Primarily affects older, lower canopy foliage under alternating wet and dry conditions.

Treatment Strategy: Practice 2-3 year crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops, eradicate volunteer plants, and apply protective fungicides (e.g., chlorothalonil, mancozeb) when early lesions appear.
Oomycete Pathogen

Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

Extremely aggressive oomycete pathogen producing rapid foliar collapse. Shows water-soaked lesions that turn purplish-black with delicate white downy mildew on the abaxial (underside) leaf surface during high humidity.

Emergency Response: Plant certified clean seed tubers, destroy infected cull piles immediately, avoid overhead irrigation, and deploy systemic fungicides (e.g., metalaxyl, cymoxanil) at initial signs of local outbreak.
